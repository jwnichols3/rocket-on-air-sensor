On-Air Light & Display Hardware - Option Slate

17 ranked options across consumer, prosumer and production tiers for rocket-on-air-sensor. Five parallel segment researchers, then a judge that re-verified every ranking-decisive claim against the primary source.

Researched 2026-08-10 · supersedes the 2026-08-05 pass · GitHub issue #1 · source of record: docs/research/2026-08-10-onair-light-hardware-slate.md

What we're scoring against

R1 · Must

Wireless - Wi-Fi or Bluetooth/BLE. No GPIO wiring to the receiver.

R2 · Strong preference

Battery operated. No mains cord at the light's location.

R3 · Bonus

Family offers both a wireless and a wired variant, ideally same API.

R4 · Extra credit

Pollable for genuine status - a real device read, not an echo of the last write.

R5 · Preference

Local-network control. Cloud-required is a strong negative.

R6 · Preference

Node driver on macOS/Linux, ideally zero new production dependencies.

Scoring: R1 is a gate, not points. R2 battery 0-4, R3 wired sibling 0-1, R4 pollable 0-3, R5 local 0-2, R6 zero-dep driver 0-2. Max 12. Ranking modifier from the repo invariant - false OFF is worse than false ON: a light that dies mid-meeting or lags a call start by minutes fails regardless of score.

The picks

Top pick
BUSY Bar
$199

The only device that satisfies wireless + battery + wired sibling + genuine read-back + local-only + zero-dep driver all at once. Verified from its own OpenAPI 3.1 spec. Also a display, so it subsumes /display rather than sitting beside it.

Runner-up
Ulanzi TC001 + AWTRIX 3
$45.99

77% of the read-back for 23% of the price. You pay in a one-time ESP32 flash and a ~5 h battery that is really a backup.

Cheapest viable
Athom WLED Slim
$11.85 · ~$30 all-in

+ a short WS2812B strip and a USB power bank. The whole Athom family shares the API, so form factor stays a $15 reversible decision. Or the $14.35 Tasmota plug for the dumbest possible contract.

Spend more, get more
Shelly 1 Gen4 + ON AIR sign
$27.99 + $149.95

The only build where confirmed is a physical current measurement. Catches a burnt-out sign or an unplugged wall-wart - nothing else on the slate can see that. Fails battery completely.

Structural tensions

Forced choices, not hedges. The hardware pick settles all three.

1. Battery XOR genuine confirmed

Held independently across all five research segments. Every device with a vendor-documented on/off read-back is mains - Shelly, LaMetric, ControlByWeb, Vestaboard, Athom bulbs, Tasmota plugs. Every purpose-built battery busylight is write-only by design - confirmed from Luxafor's own webhook docs: three write actions, no read endpoint of any kind. Broadcast tally is the same for an architectural reason - TSL UMD, Art-Net and sACN are all unidirectional. Exactly two products break it: BUSY Bar ($199) and My Bike's Got LED ($50, single-digit stock).

2. Battery XOR latency (push vs pull)

Long ESP32-class battery life requires deep sleep at ~10 µA, and a sleeping device cannot be pushed to - it must poll, so state-change lag equals the poll interval. TRMNL's headline "three months per charge" is quoted at the vendor's default refresh (docs example: 1800 s). Drive it at 30 s and the battery claim collapses to a number nobody publishes. Minutes of lag violates the false-OFF invariant. Upside worth naming: client-pull is the only way to get an honest signal out of a write-only device class, and it costs less code than any driver - one route.

3. Battery-save modes silently break automation

Govee's own product page: "When the adapter is unplugged, Battery save mode turns on automatically - disabling Alexa, Google Assistant, Matter and Wi-Fi. To re-enable them, manually turn off Battery save mode in the app." A light you wake by hand cannot be driven by an automated on-air signal. No other vendor answers this question at all - not Philips, not WiZ, not Nanoleaf.

Ask before buying any battery smart lamp: "Does it answer the network while running on battery, off the charger, with no app open?" Not answerable from any spec sheet, and decisive. Govee documents the wrong answer; everyone else is silent.

Suggested buy-and-test sequence

  1. Now, $0. Keep /display running (D-9). Sub-second via SSE, and its open-SSE-subscriber count is already a real liveness read. It stays the fallback.
  2. Now, $14.35-$27.99. One Athom Tasmota plug or Shelly 1 Gen4 + a dumb lamp. Build the real LightDriver against it - this retires D-12's "confirmed stays unknown" for fifteen dollars, on a device that will never go EOL. Success criterion: GET /status returns confirmed: "on" from a device read, and "unknown" within one poll interval when the lamp is unplugged.
  3. Week 1, $45.99. Ulanzi TC001, flashed with AWTRIX 3. Measure the two things nobody publishes: does it answer HTTP on battery with no cord, and real runtime at low brightness on a static ON AIR frame. If the first fails, you learn the Tension-3 lesson for $46 instead of $199.
  4. Week 2-4, $199 - only if step 3's form factor is right. Order the BUSY Bar (14-21 business days). Enable the Wi-Fi HTTP API over USB first; design the 409 priority handling in from day one.
  5. Finish, $149.95 - optional. Put the ON AIR sign on the Shelly from step 2 as the permanent household fixture, with the battery device as the portable desk indicator. Two lights, one driver interface, one intended state.

Open questions that gate specific purchases
  • Hue Go - battery runtime, and whether the Zigbee radio stays alive on battery. Philips's support page renders the FAQ questions but not the answers; one unread question is "What is the difference between standby and sleep mode?" Gates #5.
  • WiZ Mobile - radio-on-battery behaviour, and WiZ's own statement on local communication (help-centre page 403s). The UDP:38899 protocol has no official spec. Gates #8.
  • Cuebi - what UDP port and address/index does it listen on in TSL mode, and can Roland Smart Tally mode be pointed at an arbitrary IP? A yes to the second makes Cuebi a top-5 candidate with zero driver code; a no to both makes it unbuyable. Gates #17.
  • BUSY Bar - runtime at our duty cycle (dim, mostly-static ON AIR frame), and how to claim a display priority high enough that POST /display/draw never 409s.
  • Shelly 1 Gen4 - does the plain Gen4 report apower, or is a 1PM needed? Documented "shown if applicable".
  • Umbra Cono - whether a Thread border router exists in the house at all. matter.js is a controller, not a border router.
  • Ulanzi - whether flashing AWTRIX voids warranty, and real runtime at low brightness.
  • Embrava - everything. Needs a human with a browser.
